# Canonical candidate possession contract

Date: 2026-11-02. Version: `canonical_candidate_v7`. Status: implemented candidate, not independently validated. This contract is separate from the historical `period_start_v2` parser and changes no legacy release.

## API and provenance

`whistle_research.possessions.reconstruct(actions, game_id, home_team_id, away_team_id, source_sha256=None)` returns frozen dataclasses, serializable with `dataclasses.asdict`. Explicit home/away metadata is mandatory. The input mappings are not changed. Each normalized event retains its original zero-based source row, action ID, source ordering value, labels, source possession/team, scores and complete canonical JSON mapping. Sorting uses source order, not source row position; references preserve both.

A supplied SHA256 must have 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters. `provenance_status=declared_unverified` means the caller supplied a digest, not that this parser verified source bytes. Without a digest it is `missing`. A source ledger must verify the exact source bytes and provenance separately. `eligible` always remains false; `audit_status=blocked_pending_independent_audit` cannot be overridden by synthetic tests or parser agreement.

Malformed clocks, invalid numeric fields, uncorroborated clock/period regressions, repeated action IDs (including identical copies), repeated ordering values and non-game teams raise `ValueError`. Unsupported semantic labels remain in the event ledger with explicit blocking `ParseIssue` records. No semantic error is replaced by a negative foul target.

## Candidate control and boundaries

Possessions group explicit source control with observed terminal state. A made field goal, offensive foul, turnover or ordinary final made free throw can end a group, even when the next legal group has the same source owner. A same-clock defensive foul/free throw after a made field goal can continue an and-one. A missed final free throw continues until a subsequent observed control event. Defensive rebounds belong to the gaining team's new group; offensive rebounds continue the old group. Jump control can transfer ownership. Period end closes a group regardless of owner.

A terminal event stays pending until another control event or period-end evidence, allowing intervening administrative events and an and-one continuation. An offensive foul followed by one same-clock, same-actor/source-owner turnover explicitly labeled `offensive foul` is one possession loss, preserving both source rows and the offensive-foul boundary. Other turnover labels or later clocks do not qualify for this pairing. Timeout, substitution, replay, block and technical foul/free throw rows cannot create groups or transfer live control. They remain attached to the active group; rows outside any group appear explicitly in `unassigned_action_numbers`. Every normalized event occurs exactly once in a group or that list. `boundary_reason` documents why the group closed. A period change without an explicit end row closes the previous group with `missing_period_end` audit uncertainty.

Recognized ordinary defensive personal/shooting/loose-ball labels count only when the actor is the defense. Offensive and technical fouls do not count. Double-personal fouls require audit. Special foul labels (clear path, flagrant, away from play and take/transition take) preserve the foul event and retained-ball sequence, but mark the group for audit because seasonal rule/entitlement adjudication is not implemented. Unknown foul, shot-result, rebound and free-throw labels, unclear ownership, unsupported violations and unexplained transfers similarly create blocking audit flags. A defensive-rebound actor/source-owner conflict quarantines both adjacent control groups; unresolved foul actor/defense attribution and rebound control conflicts also make subsequent affected cumulative counts missing. Their missing targets propagate through subsequent history windows. These groups have `foul_indicator=None`; `counted_foul_action_numbers` remains an inspectable provisional event classification. `defense_counted_fouls` tracks this research classification, not official team-penalty state or bonus eligibility.

End of input does not establish end of game. Without a period-end marker, the final group has `complete=False`, `boundary_reason=input_exhausted`, `provisional_final_possession` and a missing target, even when its last row is a made shot. An appended and-one or continuation could change that group. A period-end marker establishes only the observed period boundary, not whole-game completeness or schedule coverage.

A made field goal and one corroborated defensive-goaltending companion share a group. Pairing requires the terminal shot's exact `defensivegoaltending` qualifier (a list token or comma-separated archive token), valid offensive shot actor, matching period/clock/source offense, and an opposing violation actor with subtype `defensive goaltending`. The made-basket boundary remains pending, including a subsequent and-one. Uncorroborated, mismatched or repeated companions do not qualify. In v3/v4, this exact companion receives the nonblocking `corroborated_goaltending_companion` reason rather than a generic violation flag. Other unresolved evidence still makes the target missing; the game remains ineligible.

## Feature availability

`start_action_number` is the first retrospective member of a group. It is distinct from `start_state.reference_action_number`, which names the prior recorded event. The start state snapshots scores and counted-foul history before processing the new group's event; at a period transition its clock is the regulation/OT period length. Missing initial scores stay missing. Missing period-start evidence or an unresolved foul classification makes affected cumulative counts missing, rather than zero; unknown classifications propagate into later history. The previous two indicators use global completed group order across periods.

This is a recorded-feed, before-event reference, not proof of a live prediction opportunity: offense identity and eventual boundaries derive from source events, and corrected feeds are retrospective. It does not assert that source control was observable before the reference event. No model eligibility follows from these snapshots. Unknown history remains missing, and before-state prefix values do not change when future suffix events are appended. Actual live-control timing and real-time source availability require separate evidence.

## Exact descriptive metric

`whistle_research.metrics.calculate_wmi(fouls, game_id=...)` requires a single explicitly identified game's binary integer sequence. Missing, nonbinary or floating-point inputs raise `ValueError`. Callers must establish completeness, eligible outcomes and single-game provenance; this function cannot detect concatenation hidden under a fabricated ID.

The metric uses global two-group past/future windows, crosses period and team boundaries and truncates at game edges: `L=any(previous two F)`, `N=any(next two F)`, `M=F*(1+N)`. The result retains row values, exact integer group counts, sums, foul counts and follow-up counts. Rational arithmetic computes ratios before float serialization. Empty groups, zero baseline and empty conditional foul support produce missing values with specific reason codes. WMI can equal zero even when the continuation conditional is undefined. No imputation, smoothing, pseudocount or uncertainty interval is applied. Future `N` is retrospective and cannot be a predictor.

## Candidate-v3/v4 semantic correction (2026-11-02)

The reason for this change is demonstrated overclassification of source administrative and explicitly attributed violation rows, plus special foul meaning stored in the archive descriptor rather than subtype. Original event labels and JSON remain unchanged. New semantic reason records are nonblocking only where the following narrow evidence is present:

- `violation/delay-of-game` with the exact `team` qualifier token is administrative (`administrative_delay_of_game`). It neither creates a group nor switches ownership, including when its recorded source owner differs from the active group. A separate technical foul/free throw remains separately recorded; no penalty attempt is invented.
- `violation/kicked ball` with explicit actor and source offense receives `classified_kicked_ball`. A defensive actor retains the source offense; an offensive actor sets the `offensive_kicked_ball` boundary. One same-clock, same-actor/source-owner `turnover/offensive-kicked-ball` companion shares that loss. Missing actors, unknown ownership, conflicting prior control and other violation types keep their existing blocking uncertainty.
- A defensive personal foul with exact descriptor `clear-path` or `transition take` enters the existing retained-ball path and still receives `retained_ball_requires_audit`. A later-clock ordinary defensive foul starts a new entitlement and clears the old retained state; same-clock uncertainty is preserved. A personal foul descriptor `away-from-play` receives `special_descriptor_requires_audit` without forcing retained control: the archive also uses it after early-period made baskets, while the formal special rule has timing/throw-in conditions. No descriptor asserts complete free-throw evidence, and bare `take` descriptors are not normalized by guesswork.

Historical primary support is the [NBA 2020–21 rulebook](https://ak-static.cms.nba.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/25/2021/01/2020-2021-NBA-Rule-Book.pdf), Rule 10 IV (kicks), Rule 11 (awarded-basket procedure), and Rule 12A II (delay). The [NBA 2023–24 rulebook](https://official.nba.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2023/10/2023-24-NBA-Season-Official-Playing-Rules.pdf), Rule 12B X–XI, supports distinguishing away-from-play and transition take categories. These editions support candidate category handling, not a season-by-season rules audit or footage validation. Lane, double-lane, jump-ball, unpaired goaltending and other unrecognized violations remain unresolved.

`NormalizedEvent.clock` remains the immutable recorded source timestamp. `BeforeState.clock` remains a prior-record reference, not an independently reconstructed live-state clock. V3/v4 retained every strict clock quarantine. V5 recognizes one explicit source timing pattern: a traveling turnover recorded at zero, immediately followed in source order by a same-period `instantreplay/request`, exact descriptor `support`, exact raw `value` of `Travel with time on the clock`, and source possession belonging to the opposite team. That replay timestamp can rise above zero. Neither event clock is rewritten, and `source_clock_correction_requires_audit` records the preceding action and both clocks as blocking uncertainty. A later group can reference the replay timestamp as recorded source state; it is not independently verified live time. Unknown/mismatched replay values, nontravel turnovers, period reversals and all other clock rises still raise. No administrative-time blanket exemption or clock sorting is used. The other enriched scenarios remain unresolved.

Fresh v3/v4/v5 diagnostics and exact v2 count deltas are recorded in `research/rewrite_completion_2026_11_02/parser_changes.json` and `.md`. The prior v1/v2/v3/v4 reports and their parser bindings remain historical evidence.

The initial v3 diagnostic exposed an overbroad away-from-play mapping and persistent retained entitlement. V4 fixes those two demonstrated defects with source-backed regression neighborhoods (`0022000018` and `0022000045`); the initial v3 report is preserved and is not the final parser result.

Candidate-v5 clock evidence (2026-11-02): archive game `0022000925`, actions 337–339, explicitly records the supported travel timing review and subsequent play at 0.2 seconds. The 2020–21 rulebook, Rule 5 III(a)(2), permits added time when the whistle precedes zero. This supports retaining the source correction as auditable evidence; it does not independently certify the clock or resolve the game’s other control conflicts.


## Candidate-v6 source semantic corrections (2026-11-02)

Reason: hash-verified archive neighborhoods reproduce control and classification defects that v5's generic audit flags did not distinguish. Original event JSON, source row/order, possession field and timestamps remain unchanged. The new evidence packet is `research/parser_followthrough_2026_11_02/semantic_changes.json`; it binds raw neighborhoods to archive SHA256, CSV row, game/action IDs and historical home/away metadata. This is implementation evidence, not independent film validation.

- An explicitly recovered jump ball uses the recovery actor as the candidate gaining team. The archive can retain the pre-toss source possession field (`0022000003`, actions 566/571); `recovered_jump_actor_control` records that discrepancy without changing the source field. Missing recovery actors remain blocking. An explicitly attributed `violation/jumpball` awards control to the opposing team: an offensive violator closes with `offensive_jump_violation`, while a defensive violator retains the source offense (`0022000248`, actions 7/8; `0022000033`, action 156). Missing actors/owners and other violation subtypes remain unresolved.
- A rebound with both exact qualifier tokens `team` and `deadball` is administrative (`administrative_deadball_rebound`) only when the nearest preceding control/scoring row is a same-clock, same-period free throw that is nonfinal, technical, or under active retained entitlement. This includes a rebound after a technical free throw (`0022400921`, actions 62–64). It cannot establish live possession. An ordinary final-miss defensive rebound keeps its control-award meaning even with both tokens (`0022300470`, actions 652–654). Other rebounds keep the existing control and conflict handling. Administrative rows outside an active group remain explicitly unassigned.
- One ordinary defensive foul recorded after a final made free throw can be a delayed entitlement companion when source offense and clock agree, its exact `Nfreethrow` token agrees with that terminal attempt's sequence total, and the group has no already-recorded free-throw entitlement foul. Double/special descriptors, conflicting actors, different clocks and repeated companions do not qualify. Both rows stay in the same group and the `final_made_free_throw` boundary stays pending (`0022000013`, actions 502/505/500/506). This uses only prior source events; no event is reordered.
- One defensive-goaltending record immediately after a recorded made ordinary free throw, ignoring administrative rows, stays with that awarded point when source offense, clock and opposing actor agree (`0022000114`, actions 360–363). Final free throws keep their pending terminal boundary; nonfinal throws continue the entitlement. Repeated/mismatched companions remain unresolved. A defensive lane record immediately following a known awarded-foul entitlement or valid made same-clock free throw cannot transfer control (`0022000002`, actions 590/608/592/609). A preceding missed, unknown or malformed free throw does not qualify: required replacement-attempt evidence remains unknown. Intervening technical free throws cannot be skipped to find an earlier made ordinary throw. No substitute attempt is invented. Offensive lane, double-lane and uncorroborated violations remain blocking.
- Exact personal-foul descriptors `flagrant-type-1`, `flagrant-type-2` and `punching` enter the retained-ball audit path, alongside the existing exact special categories (`0022400875`, actions 544/550/551). The retained-ball audit flag remains blocking. Personal fouls with exact descriptor `double` enter `double_foul_requires_audit` before ordinary defensive attribution; neither row is silently counted as a normal defensive foul, and future affected counts remain missing. Special rule/season adjudication remains unverified.
- `memo` is a non-control source note. Its `value` field can describe officiating, injuries or statistical milestones. Every memo receives blocking `source_note_requires_audit`, makes its active group's target missing and propagates missing affected foul counts. It cannot transfer possession, and it cannot silently establish a negative outcome (`0022000932`, actions 595/596/599). This reclassifies the unsupported record without claiming its implied events are resolved. V6 still quarantined memo clock rises; the v7 correction below supersedes that behavior.

Primary support: the [2020–21 NBA rulebook](https://ak-static.cms.nba.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/25/2021/01/2020-2021-NBA-Rule-Book.pdf), Rules 6 (dead ball/jump balls), 9 (free-throw violations), 10 V (jump-ball violations) and 12B II/IV/VI (special and double fouls); current official [Rule 6](https://official.nba.com/rule-no-6-putting-ball-in-play-live-dead-ball/), [Rule 9](https://official.nba.com/rule-no-9-free-throws-and-penalties/) and [Rule 10](https://official.nba.com/rule-no-10-violations-and-penalties/) corroborate those category distinctions. Source labels establish candidate classification only, not correct officiating, a complete seasonal entitlement audit or a validated prediction opportunity. Special retained-ball and away-from-play flags, ownership conflicts, unmatched lane/double-lane violations and unsupported clocks remain explicit uncertainty. No game becomes eligible.


The initial v6 all-game diagnostic exposed a second jump-control boundary defect: a recovered held ball can precede the losing jumper's recorded turnover. One immediately following `lost ball` or `bad pass` turnover can be a jump-loss companion when its explicit source owner and actor team are the pre-jump source offense, its `personId` exactly matches that jump's `jumpBallLostPersonId`, and no live control/scoring row intervenes. The original owner remains in the event ledger; the companion stays with the recovering team's group without ending that group's live control or manufacturing another loss. A second turnover, different actor/label, missing jump identity or intervening play does not qualify. `paired_jump_loss_companion` identifies the exact prior jump; differing nonincreasing clocks add blocking `jump_loss_timing_requires_audit` rather than silently assuming contemporaneous events (`0022000005`, 111/114/115; `0022000003`, 298/301/302). This correction does not assume every later turnover after a jump is a companion.

V6 adds one further prior-evidence clock pattern: a missed offensive shot recorded at zero, its same-offense team offensive rebound at zero with matching `shotActionNumber`, then a same-period supported replay request with exact `value` `Foul occurred prior to expiration of time` and the same explicit offense (`0022100865`, 176–179). Both source clocks remain unchanged, and blocking `source_clock_correction_requires_audit` remains. No foul/attempt is synthesized and no future suffix is needed to recognize this recorded review. The reused text following a defensive rebound at 0.1 (`0022100206`, 159) or a midperiod timeout (`0022101229`, 562) remains quarantined. Rule 5 III(a)(2) in the 2020–21 rulebook supports restoring time when a whistle preceded zero; it does not verify this source sequence. The actual extended-game-clock anomaly in `0022301177` remains quarantined; no duration is removed or events reordered.
