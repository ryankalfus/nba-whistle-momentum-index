Research direction

One question to organize the project.

After accounting for what was already happening in an NBA game, does recent defensive-foul history improve our ability to predict a defensive foul on the next possession?

What the data says

A small signal with important limits.

In the audited 2024–25 possession study, the immediate foul ratio was 0.969 and the conditional continuation ratio was 1.011. Their product was a pooled descriptive WMI of 0.980. These are research diagnostics; the public WMI remains one score per game.

Lag estimates do not show a smooth fade. Later windows often contain another foul, so one call’s independent influence cannot be read directly from those coefficients.

Read the full hypothesis report

Interpretation boundary

Recorded foul sequences can reflect tactics, teams, score, bonus, opportunities, and officiating. They do not by themselves establish which calls were right or why they were made.

Matched models were evaluated on later seasons using the same games and context variables; one model also included past foul history.

In 2024–25 regular-season games, adding foul history changed held-out log loss from 0.443315 to 0.443260, an improvement of 0.000055. The game-bootstrap 95% interval for the change was −0.000107 to −0.000007. The gain is measurable but small.

Method

How WMI is made.

WMI uses the last two and next two possessions in global game order. Because it looks ahead, a completed game is required.

Game-level formula
WMI = mean(Mt | Lt = 1) ÷ mean(Mt | Lt = 0)

Mt = Ft + FtNt

Lt
A counted defensive foul in either of the prior two possessions.
Ft
A counted defensive foul in the current possession.
Nt
A counted defensive foul in either of the next two possessions.

WMI > 1 means a higher observed ratio after recent fouls; WMI < 1 means lower. Near 1 is a small observed difference, not proof of equivalence. Per-game confidence intervals are withheld because tested coverage was inconsistent.
